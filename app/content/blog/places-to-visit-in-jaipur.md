*Padharo mhare des*, “welcome to my land”, is how Rajasthan greets its guests. As the state capital, Jaipur welcomes travellers from across India and around the world. Here are the places we send our own guests to, and how to get the most out of a weekend or a longer stay.

## The essential Jaipur

**Amer Fort.** About 19 km from the palace, the honey-coloured citadel above Maota Lake is the jewel of the Kachhwaha Rajputs. Go early in the morning, before the crowds and the heat.

**Jaigarh Fort.** Built in the 18th century by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II as the protector of Amer and Jaipur, Jaigarh sits on Cheel ka Teela, the “hill of eagles”, with sweeping views over the city. It is also home to the Jaivana, once one of the largest cannons on wheels in the world.

**Nahargarh Fort.** Another of Sawai Jai Singh II’s forts, set against the Aravalli hills. Don’t miss the artwork and craftsmanship of the Madhavendra Bhawan, and stay for sunset over the Pink City.

**Jal Mahal.** The “water palace” floats in the middle of Man Sagar Lake on the road between Amer and the city. It is best photographed from the promenade in soft evening light.

**City Palace.** Begun by Sawai Jai Singh II in the 18th century and added to by later rulers, the City Palace blends Rajput, Mughal and European architecture. Its museum, courtyards and painted gateways deserve an unhurried afternoon.

**Jantar Mantar.** A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the largest of Sawai Jai Singh II’s astronomical observatories, completed in the 1730s. The monumental instruments still tell the time by the sun.

**Hawa Mahal.** Built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh, the “palace of winds” is a five-storey façade of pink sandstone jharokhas. From inside you can look down on the bazaar, just as the royal ladies once did.

**Albert Hall Museum.** Set in Ram Niwas Garden, this Indo-Saracenic building holds rare collections of jewellery, paintings, musical instruments and arms.

**Kanak Vrindavan.** A peaceful garden and temple complex at the foot of the hills near Amer, with the much-loved Govind Devji temple close by in the city.

Also worth a visit: Birla Mandir, the royal cenotaphs at Gaitore, and the old bazaars of Johari and Bapu.

## Planning your days

- **Morning:** Amer and Jaigarh while it is cool.
- **Afternoon:** City Palace, Jantar Mantar and Hawa Mahal, all within walking distance of each other in the walled city.
- **Evening:** Nahargarh for sunset, or come back to the palace for a swim and dinner under the sky.

The best season to visit is October to March. Monsoon (July–September) turns the Aravallis green and is beautiful, but plan for showers.

## A palace base in the Aravallis

After a day in the city, there is nothing better than returning to calm. The Vijayran Palace sits in the foothills of the Aravallis at Kukas, close to Amer but far from the noise. Expect carved jharokhas, courtyard suites, private-pool villas and one of the largest pools in Jaipur.
