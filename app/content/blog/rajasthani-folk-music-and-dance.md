When the sun sets over the Aravallis and the bonfire is lit, the palace lawns fill with the sound of Rajasthan. Here is a short guide to what you will hear and see at a folk evening.

## The Ravanhatha

One of India’s oldest bowed instruments, the Ravanhatha has a bamboo neck, a coconut-shell body covered in skin and a horsehair bow hung with tiny bells. Its voice is raw and haunting. Played by travelling musicians for centuries, it is often called the ancestor of the violin.

## Kalbeliya

The dance of the Kalbeliya community is fast, fluid and hypnotic. Dancers in swirling black skirts embroidered with mirrors move like serpents to the sound of the *been* and the *dhol*. Kalbeliya songs and dances are inscribed on UNESCO’s list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

## Ghoomar

Ghoomar is the graceful dance of Rajasthan’s royal courts and festivals. Dancers turn in slow, perfect circles, and their ghagras open like blossoms. It is performed at weddings, at Teej and Gangaur, and wherever there is something to celebrate.

## Other sounds of the desert

- **Dholak and dhol:** the heartbeat of every celebration
- **Khartal:** wooden clappers that drive the rhythm
- **Kamaicha and sarangi:** bowed strings of the Manganiyar and Langa musicians
