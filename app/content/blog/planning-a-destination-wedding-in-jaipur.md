A Jaipur palace wedding is a dream for many couples, and for good reason. Few places mix heritage architecture, mountain sunsets and warm hospitality the way Rajasthan does. This guide walks you through planning a celebration at The Vijayran Palace.

## Choose your season

October to March is wedding season in Jaipur: clear skies, cool evenings and golden light for photographs. Lawn events are at their best then. Monsoon months can be beautiful, but plan an indoor option such as the air-conditioned **Raj Mahal**.

## Match each ceremony to a space

The palace has seven celebration spaces, so a multi-day wedding never feels repetitive:

| Ceremony | Our suggestion | Guests |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome lunch | Royal Courtyard | 100–150 |
| Haldi | Trantal, the main pool area | 150–200 |
| Mehendi | Haveli Chowk | 100–150 |
| Sangeet | Raj Mahal | 250–300 |
| Pheras | Char Bagh | 200–250 |
| Reception | Rang Mahal or Vijayran Mahal Bagh | 400–1,500 |

Use the [venue finder on our weddings page](/weddings) to match spaces to your guest list.

## A three-day plan

**Day one:** guests arrive to a welcome lunch in the Royal Courtyard, then an evening Mehendi in the aangan with folk musicians.

**Day two:** Haldi by the pool in the morning, and a musical night or Sangeet with a DJ or live band after dark.

**Day three:** Pheras at sunset in Char Bagh with the Aravallis behind you, followed by a grand reception on the lawns.

## Make it unmistakably Rajasthani

- An exclusive **cultural show** for your guests, with folk songs, Kalbeliya and Ghoomar
- A **pre-wedding shoot** in the jharokhas, the painted halls and the arched corridors
- Menus that pair **Rajasthani classics** with Hyderabadi, Awadhi and Kashmiri favourites

## Questions to ask any venue

1. How many guests can stay on-site, and is there a full-buyout option?
2. Which spaces have weather back-ups?
3. Are outside decorators, caterers and DJs allowed?
4. What is included in the per-plate price?
5. What are the timings and sound restrictions for outdoor events?
